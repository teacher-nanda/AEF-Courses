📐 Grammar

Questions with be and can

Study how questions with be and can are formed.

Question wordVerbSubject
Areyouhungry?
Istherea bank near here?
CanIsit here?
Whatwasthatnoise?
Wherewereyouborn?

We make questions with the verb be and can by inverting the verb and the subject.

She is a teacher. → Is she a teacher?

He can drive. → Can he drive?

💭 Check your understanding

  • "She is a teacher." → "…she a teacher?" Which word moves, and where does it go? The verb is moves in front of the subject: Is she a teacher?
  • Do we need do / does / did in questions with be and can? No — we just invert the verb and the subject.
⚠️ Typical mistakes — students sometimes forget to invert the subject and verb.

Ana is a student? → Is Ana a student?

Where I can sit? → Where can I sit?

📐 Grammar

Questions with do / does / did

Study how questions are formed in the simple present and simple past.

Question wordAuxiliarySubjectBase form
Doyoulive with your parents?
Didyoutake a vacation last year?
Wheredoesyour sisterwork?
Whendidyoustart studying English?
Whatdidtheytalk about?

Use this word order

auxiliary + subject + base form, e.g., Did you go out last night?

or question word + auxiliary + subject + base form, e.g., Where did you go?

💭 Check your understanding

  • "Where does your sister work?" Does the main verb change to works? No — after do / does / did we use the base form: work.
  • In a question with did, what is the order of the words? (question word) + did + subject + base form.
⚠️ Typical mistakes — students might leave out the auxiliary or get the word order wrong.

You live with your parents? → Do you live with your parents?

Why she didn't like the movie? → Why didn't she like the movie?

📌 If a verb is followed by a preposition (talk about, listen to), the preposition goes at the end of the question: What did you talk about?
