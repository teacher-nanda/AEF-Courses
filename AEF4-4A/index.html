🔋 Energy

Fossil fuels, like coal and gas, will be very expensive. Most people will have installed solar panels or wind turbines on their houses or apartment buildings to generate their electricity.

♻️ Waste

People will be recycling nearly 100% of their waste (and those who don't will have to pay a fine). All stores and cafés will have stopped using plastic bags and single-use containers, like to-go coffee cups.

🚌 Transportation

Governments will have invested a lot of money in public transportation. Everyone will be riding their bikes, walking, or using the bus and train more. Low-cost airlines will have disappeared and flights will be much more expensive.

🚰 Food and water

Farmers will have stopped producing meat commercially and many kinds of fish will have died out. Fresh water will be running out in many parts of the world, and we will be getting much of our water from the ocean (through desalination plants).

🌦️ The weather

We will be having more extreme weather, and heatwaves, hurricanes, floods, etc., will be frequent occurrences. Many ski resorts will have closed because of a lack of winter snow, and some low-lying beaches and vacation resorts will have disappeared completely.

📐 Grammar

Finished, or still happening?

Read each example from the infographic, then answer the questions below it.

⏱️ Future Perfect 🔄 Future Continuous

⏱️ Future Perfect

The rain will have stopped by this afternoon.

Check your understanding

1. Are we talking about a point before this afternoon, exactly this afternoon, or after?

Before this afternoon — "by" means at the latest.

2. Do we know the exact time the rain stops?

No — we only know it's finished by a certain point in time.

3. At that point in time, is the action finished or still happening?

Finished — the rain has already stopped by then.
Some people think that sea levels will have risen by as much as 3 feet in 50 years.
Laura won't have arrived before dinner, so I'll leave some food on the stove for her.
When will they have learned enough English to be able to communicate fluently?

🔄 Future Continuous

Don't call between 7:00 and 8:30 because we'll be having dinner then.

Check your understanding

1. Does dinner start before 7:00, at 7:00, or sometime between 7:00 and 8:30?

Before — it's already started, and it's still going on during that time.

2. At any point between 7:00 and 8:30, is dinner finished or still happening?

Still happening — it's in progress at that time.

3. Do we know exactly when dinner will finish?

No — we just know it's in progress at that time.
Good luck with your test tomorrow. I'll be thinking of you.
Will you be waiting for me when I get off the train?
This time tomorrow, I'll be sitting on the beach watching the sunset.
🔍 Compare: future simple vs. future continuous
Come at around 7:30.
We'll have dinner at 8:00. (future simple — dinner starts at 8:00)
Don't call between 7:00 and 8:30 because
we'll be having dinner. (future continuous — at 8:00, dinner is already in progress)
🔗 Also like the present continuous:
we sometimes use the future continuous to talk about things that are already planned or decided:
I'll be going to the supermarket later. Do you want anything?
📐 Grammar

Building the sentence

⏱️ Future Perfect

will have + past participle

Negative: won't have + past participle.

Question: invert the subject and will / won't.
Will they have finished?

Frequently used with time expressions like by Saturday / March / 2030, etc., or in two weeks / months, etc. (by + a time expression = at the latest.)

🔄 Future Continuous

will be + verb + -ing

Negative: won't be + verb + -ing.
You don't need to get up early.
We won't be leaving until about 9:30.

Question: invert the subject and will / won't.

📐 Grammar

Natural Speech

In fast, natural speech, both tenses are almost always contracted and linked. Listen for the stress falling on the main verb, not on will.

⏱️ Future Perfect

🎧 Listen

The rain 'll have stopped by this afternoon.

will have contracts to 'll have, and the h in have often disappears completely. Stress falls on the past participle, not on will.

🔄 Future Continuous

🎧 Listen

Don't call between 7:00 and 8:30 because we'll be having dinner then.

will be contracts to 'll be and links smoothly into the -ing verb. Stress falls on the -ing verb, not on will or be.

🔍 definitely, probably, and likely / unlikely

Umberto Crespo Palmarito

Here, the rainy season used to start in March and the rain stopped in November. Now, the heavy rain only starts in June. Years ago, it would be pouring rain every day. And now there can be a week, 15 days, without any rain. My grandfather and my father lived their life according to the weather because it was like a clock: it was never wrong. We used to say that September 21st was the day the weather changed. And now people don't say it. It's completely different from before.

Nadine Lefort

For many years, we had less snow in the winter, and then this past year we had an extreme winter — freezing, with terrible blizzards — so weather patterns are changing and it's less predictable. Another thing I notice is that the coasts seem to be eroding much more quickly than they were in the past. It's sad, because so many beautiful properties and parks are right on the coast and it will be a shame to see them gone. People are saying that they'd never buy or build in those places because they'll be gone in the future.

Harou Abass Hadiza

When I was in elementary school, my friends and I used to go to the river. It was green, and the air was cool and fresh. Some of us were afraid to go far from the riverbank when we were swimming, because the river was deep and had a strong current. However, in the last few years, we've been experiencing increasingly hot weather — extreme heat. Now the river isn't so deep, and it's dusty and dirty. Air quality in my city has also declined. There is more dust, due to desertification.

Jordan Hamada

There hasn't been a big snowstorm here for over ten years. This area is known for its rain, and there hasn't been much for the past few months, and I'm pretty surprised, because it's been so dry this winter. It's definitely not something I think about all the time, but I've seen some articles recently talking about how Los Angeles and Manhattan will eventually be under water, possibly in our lifetime, or the next generation's lifetime, and that makes it seem very real — that's definitely a scary thought.

✅ Already discussed

    🔥 Modifiers with Strong Adjectives

    Level 1

    very / really / extremely / incredibly / unbelievably + normal adjective

    "It was incredibly cold."

    Level 2

    Strong adjectives (already extreme): boiling, freezing, scorching, exhausted, terrified…

    "It's freezing today."

    Level 3

    absolutely + strong adjective

    "It was absolutely freezing."

    ⚠️ Strong adjectives already mean "very ___", so we don't say very freezing or really boiling.

    ⭐ Lesson Review

    What have we learned?

    📐 Grammar — Future Perfect & Continuous

    will have + past participle will be + verb-ing

    Future perfect = finished by a future time. Future continuous = in progress at a future time.

    📚 Vocabulary — Weather

    chilly / freezing / boiling damp / drizzling / showers heavy / icy / changeable

    🔊 Pronunciation — Vowel Sounds

    Same spelling, different sounds: ow, ea, i, oo can all be pronounced more than one way (e.g., owl vs. snow; tree vs. weather).

    HW9 — Write a paragraph about the weather

    Write a paragraph about the weather in your country in January, April, July, and October.

    In my country, the weather changes quite a lot throughout the year. In January, it's usually freezing, and some mornings it's actually below zero, especially in the mountains — the roads get icy, so people drive carefully. By April, the weather becomes much more changeable: we get bright, sunny mornings followed by sudden showers in the afternoon, and there's often a cool breeze blowing in from the coast. July is the hottest month — it's typically warm, sometimes even boiling in the cities, and the air can feel quite humid near the coast, though inland it stays fairly dry. We occasionally get a heat wave that lasts for several days. By October, things start to cool down again: mornings are often damp and drizzling, with thick fog rolling in from the hills, but there are still a few mild, settled afternoons before winter really begins.
