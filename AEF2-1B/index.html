💭 CCQs

  • Does a lazy person work hard? No. A lazy person doesn't want to work. The opposite is hardworking.
  • Is a shy person comfortable with new people? No — he / she can't talk easily to people he / she doesn't know. The opposite is extroverted.
  • Does a generous person like giving things to people? Yes. The opposite is cheap (he / she never pays for anything).
  • Is a talkative person quiet? No — a talkative person talks a lot (he never stops talking). The opposite is quiet.
  • If someone is funny, what do you do when you are with him / her? You laugh! The opposite is serious.
  • Is a smart person good at learning things? Yes. The opposite is stupid.
  • Is a kind person good to other people? Yes. The opposite is unkind (in American English, also mean).
  • Is a friendly person open and warm? Yes. The opposite is unfriendly.

📐 Grammar

Simple present: what does it mean?

Look at these sentences from the article. Answer the questions about the meaning.

They often go out together in the evening.

  • Are they going out right now? No — it's not happening at this moment.
  • Does it happen again and again? Yes — it's a habit / routine. How often? Often (not always, not rarely).

Charlotte thinks that Clint needs a new partner.

  • Is this a short action or a situation? A situation (a state) — and it is true now and in general.

Charlotte doesn't want her dad to end up alone.

  • Does Charlotte want her dad to be alone? No. We use doesn't + base form (want) for negatives with he / she / it.

Does Clint like internet dating?

  • How do we make a question with he / she / it? Does + subject + base form. The verb doesn't change: Does he like…?
📌 We use the simple present for things we do every day / week / year, or for things that are generally true or always happen.

Adverbs and expressions of frequency

How often? Study the adverbs and the rules.

always100%
usually80%
often65%
sometimes45%
hardly ever15%

1 We often use the simple present with adverbs of frequency (always, often, sometimes, usually, hardly ever, never).

We often go out on Friday night.
She doesn't usually study on weekends.
I'm never sick.
He's always late for work.

• Adverbs of frequency go before the main verb.

• Adverbs of frequency go after be: She's never sick. She never is sick.

• Remember to use a positive verb with never: It never rains. It doesn't never rain.

2 Expressions of frequency (every day, once a week, twice a week, etc.) usually go at the end of a sentence.

She gets up early every day.
We have English classes twice a week.

💭 Check your understanding

  • Maria never eats meat. How often does she eat meat? Zero times (0%).
  • Tom hardly ever goes to the gym. Does he go often? No — almost never (very rarely).
  • Is "I go twice a week to the gym" correct? Where does the expression usually go? Better: I go to the gym twice a week. — at the end of the sentence.
📌 once and twice are irregular: once a month, twice a year — but from three on we use times: three times a week, ten times a year. Usually, normally, and sometimes can also go at the beginning: Sometimes I get up late on Saturday.

Pronunciation of final -s and -es

The final -s is pronounced /s/ or /z/.

The final -es is pronounced /ɪz/ after c, ch, g, s, sh, x, and z.

💭 Try it!

  • Voiced sounds: put your hand on your throat and say /z/. Can you feel it vibrate? And /s/? /z/ vibrates (voiced). /s/ does not (unvoiced). A snake = /s/. A bee or fly = /z/.
  • The final s is /s/ after unvoiced sounds: /k/, /p/, /f/, /t/ (walks, stops, laughs, eats). What about plays, parties? /z/ — in all other cases the final s is voiced.
