💬 Speaking

Medical Myths or First-Aid Facts?

First aid can help treat a minor injury, or even save a life in a medical emergency. However, it’s important to know what NOT to do. Sometimes, incorrect first aid can actually be more harmful than helpful. For each question, decide which answers are myths (M) and which are facts (F). Click the button to toggle your answer.

1. What’s the first thing you should put on a burn?

a  butter
b  cool running waterwater from the faucet or tap
c  an ice pack
The correct answer is b. Run cool or lukewarm water on the burn for between 5 and 20 minutes. This will cool the skin and stop blisters from forming. a and c are myths. Putting anything that is oily on a burn can increase the risk of infection, and ice or iced water will make the damage worse.

2. How should you treat a sprained ankle?

a  put a hot, damp clothslightly wet cloth on the ankle
b  put an ice pack on the ankle
c  put the leg up, e.g., on a chair
The correct answers are b and c. Get the person to sit down and raise their leg by putting it on a chair. Then put an ice pack on the ankle. These two things will help to reduce the swelling. a is a myth. Applying heat to an area increases blood flow, which can increase swelling, so the injury will take longer to get better.

3. What’s the best thing to do for someone with hypothermia?

a  rubto press two surfaces against each other and move them backwards and forwards their arms and legs to warm them up
b  give them hot coffee
c  cover them in something warm, e.g., a coat or a blanket
The correct answer is c. Remove any wet clothes, wrap the person in something warm and dry like a coat or a blanket, especially their head, and try to protect them from the wind. a and b are myths. Rubbing causes a person to lose more heat, and although a hot drink can also help, it should be caffeine-free.

4. What’s the first thing to do if someone is choking?

a  stand behind them and pressto push something closely and firmly against something their stomach inwards
b  make them continue to cough hard
c  hit them hard on the back
The correct answers are b and c. First check the mouth and encourage them to keep coughing. If this doesn’t work, hit the person’s back hard five times. a is not the first thing you should try. Abdominal thrusts (the Heimlich manoeuvre) should only be used if the person can’t talk, cough, or breathe.

5. What’s the best way to stop a nosebleed?

a  tipto move so that one end or side is higher than the other your head forwards
b  pinchto hold something tightly between the thumb and finger the soft part of your nose
c  tipto move so that one end or side is higher than the other your head backwards
The correct answers are a and b. Pinch the soft part of your nose firmly and tip your head forwards. c is a myth. Tipping your head backwards can be dangerous if the bleeding is severe.

6. After you have cleaned a bad cut, what should you do?

a  put on a bandagea strip of cloth used for tying around a part of the body that has been hurt, in order to protect or support it
b  put on antibiotic ointment
c  leave it open to the air
The correct answers are a and b. After cleaning the cut with soap and water, put on antibiotic ointment and a bandage to stop the wound from getting infected. c is a myth. An uncovered wound is unprotected, which makes it less likely that it will heal.

📐 Grammar

Present Perfect Simple and Continuous

Present Perfect Simple
have / has + past participle
I’ve known her for years.    She’s broken her arm.
Present Perfect Continuous
have / has + been + verb+ing
I’ve been waiting for hours.    She’s been working all day.
Use SIMPLE for…
1Past experiences — with ever / never
Have you ever broken a bone?
2With yet and already
I’ve already told you. / It hasn’t arrived yet.
3With superlatives, first / last time
It’s the best book I’ve ever read.
4Nonaction verbs (be, know, like, need…)
I’ve known Keiko since I was a child.
5How much / many / how often up to now
They’ve seen each other twice this week.
Use CONTINUOUS for…
1Action verbs — unfinished actions
How long have you been waiting? (for / since)
2Repeated actions (all day, recently)
I haven’t been sleeping well recently.
3Actions just finished (present results)
I’m exhausted. I’ve been shopping all morning.
⚡ Simple or Continuous?
I’ve painted the kitchen. → completed
I’ve been painting the kitchen. → duration / may not be done
With live / work → both possible   (continuous = more temporary)
❌ I know Keiko since I was a child. ← use present perfect, not simple present

Simple — have/has + past participle
• past experiences: Have you ever broken a bone?
• with yet / already: I’ve already told you.
• non-action verbs + for/since: I’ve known her since 2010.
