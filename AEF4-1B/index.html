Myth or fact?

Is it a MYTH (not true) or a FACT (really happened)?
Choose one, then click "Reveal" to find out.

1. In 1900, three lighthouse keepers disappeared from a small island in Scotland. Nobody ever found out what happened to them.

MYTH FACT
It's a FACT.
In December 1900, three keepers — Thomas Marshall, James Ducat, and Donald MacArthur — vanished from the Flannan Isles Lighthouse in Scotland.
When a ship arrived, the light wasn't working, the beds were unmade, and a meal was left on the table.
No one was ever found, and nobody knows for sure what happened.

2. In an area of the Atlantic Ocean called the Bermuda Triangle, ships and planes disappear much more often than anywhere else in the world.

MYTH FACT
It's a MYTH.
Some famous disappearances, like a group of five Navy planes in 1945, made the Bermuda Triangle famous.
But according to the US National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA), ships and planes are lost there no more often than in any other busy area of ocean.
Storms and heavy traffic explain most cases.

3. A letter was delivered in London over 100 years after it was sent, and nobody knows how.

MYTH FACT
It's a FACT.
A letter written in 1916 was finally delivered to a flat in Crystal Palace, London, in 2021 — 105 years later!
It was written by a woman named Christabel Mennell to her friend Katie Marsh.
The Royal Mail said they don't know how or why it took so long.

4. In 1872, a ship called the Mary Celeste was found sailing alone in the Atlantic Ocean. The captain, his family, and the whole crew were never seen again — but a lifeboat was missing.

MYTH FACT
It's a FACT.
The Mary Celeste was found abandoned near the Azores with its cargo, food, and water still on board — but its lifeboat and navigation instruments were gone.
Nobody knows exactly why Captain Briggs, his family, and the crew of eight left the ship.
It's one of the most famous sea mysteries ever.

📐 Grammar

Auxiliary verbs

We use auxiliary verbs (do, have, etc.) or modal verbs (can, must, etc.) for six different jobs.
Click each one to see how it works.

Instead of repeating the whole verb phrase, just use the auxiliary.
I like cats, but my husband doesn't like cats. ✗ I like cats, but my husband doesn't. ✓
So + aux + subject responds to an affirmative statement.
Neither + aux + subject responds to a negative one — always with an affirmative auxiliary.
"I loved his latest movie." "So did I." "I haven't finished the book." "Neither have I." Neither didn't I. ✗
Respond to a statement to show that you (or someone/something else) are not the same.
"I don't like shopping online." "I do. I buy a lot of my clothes online."
A short question built from just the auxiliary + subject.
Often shows interest or surprise.
"I went to a psychic yesterday." "You did?" "I'll make dinner tonight." "You will? That's great!"
To contradict someone, in the simple present/past we add do/does/did before the main verb.
With other auxiliaries (be, have, will), just stress the auxiliary — don't contract it.
"You didn't lock the door!" "I DID lock it; I know I did."
Affirmative statement → negative tag.
Negative statement → affirmative tag.
You won't forget, will you? She can speak Italian, can't she?
🎵 Two different intonations
↘ Falling — just inviting agreement:
"It's a nice day, isn't it?"
↗ Rising — checking something you think is true, like a normal yes/no question:
"She's a painter, isn't she?"
Concept-checking questions
  • If I say "I've never seen a UFO," and you say "Neither have I" — have you seen one? No, you haven't either.
  • After "neither," do we use an affirmative or negative auxiliary? Affirmative — "Neither did I," not "Neither didn't I."
  • Is a falling-intonation tag question asking for real information, or just inviting agreement? Just inviting agreement.

📐 Grammar

The more, the merrier

Meaning

We use the + comparative adjective/adverb, or the more / less (+ noun), twice in one sentence to show that one thing depends on, or changes together with, another.

  • "The earlier we start, the sooner we'll finish." = If we start early, we'll finish early.
  • "The more money you spend now, the less you'll have for your vacation." = If you spend a lot of money now, you'll have less for your vacation.
Concept-checking questions
  • In "The bigger the animal, the more problems you have" — if the animal is small, do you have more or fewer problems? Fewer problems.
  • Do the two halves of the sentence change in the SAME direction, or OPPOSITE directions? The same direction (both go up, or both go down, together).
From the forest activity "The harder and more resistant the cup is, the stronger your relationship is."
💡 Form: The + comparative + clause 1, the + comparative + clause 2.
With uncountable/plural nouns, use the more / the less + noun.

📚 Vocabulary

Personality words

Meaning & Form

A compound adjective is made of two words joined together (often with a hyphen) to describe personality.
Some are positive, some negative, and some can be either depending on context.

  • absent-minded, easygoing, laid-back, well-balanced, open-minded, strong-willed, well-behaved, good-tempered — often positive
  • big-headed, narrow-minded, self-centered, tight-fisted, two-faced, bad-tempered — often negative
💡 Stress: in compound adjectives like these, the stress usually falls on the second word — absent-MINDed, self-CENTered, tight-FISTed.
