📐 Grammar

Auxiliary verbs: reply questions, tag questions, so / neither

Meaning

We use short forms with auxiliary verbs (do, did, have, be, can, will…) instead of repeating a whole verb — it sounds much more natural in conversation.

  • Reply questions — to show interest or surprise. A: "I saw a ghost last night." B: "You did?"
  • Tag questions — added to a statement to check information or invite agreement. Positive statement → negative tag; negative statement → positive tag. "You believe in ghosts, don't you?" / "You don't believe in UFOs, do you?"
  • So + auxiliary + subject — to say you're the same, after an affirmative statement. A: "I love a good mystery." B: "So do I."
  • Neither + auxiliary + subject — to say you're the same, after a negative statement. A: "I don't believe in ghosts." B: "Neither do I."
  • Stressed auxiliary for emphasis — to contradict or emphasize. A: "You don't really think it's true." B: "I DO think it's true!"
Concept-checking questions
  • If I say "I've never seen a UFO," and you say "Neither have I" — does that mean you HAVE seen one, or you HAVEN'T? You haven't either.
  • Is a tag question added to a positive or negative statement usually negative? Usually negative (after a positive statement).
  • If someone says "You didn't lock the door?" with rising intonation — are they asking for new information, or reacting with surprise? Reacting with surprise (a reply question).

📐 Grammar

The + comparative…, the + comparative…

Meaning

We use the + comparative adjective/adverb, or the more / less (+ noun), twice in one sentence to show that one thing depends on, or changes together with, another.

  • "The earlier we start, the sooner we'll finish." = If we start early, we'll finish early.
  • "The more money you spend now, the less you'll have for your vacation." = If you spend a lot of money now, you'll have less for your vacation.
Concept-checking questions
  • In "The bigger the animal, the more problems you have" — if the animal is small, do you have more or fewer problems? Fewer problems.
  • Do the two halves of the sentence change in the SAME direction, or OPPOSITE directions? The same direction (both go up, or both go down, together).
From the forest activity "The harder and more resistant the cup is, the stronger your relationship is."
💡 Form: The + comparative + clause 1, the + comparative + clause 2. With uncountable/plural nouns, use the more / the less + noun.

📚 Vocabulary

Compound adjectives for personality

Meaning & Form

A compound adjective is made of two words joined together (often with a hyphen) to describe personality. Some are positive, some negative, and some can be either depending on context.

  • absent-minded, easygoing, laid-back, well-balanced, open-minded, strong-willed, well-behaved, good-tempered — often positive
  • big-headed, narrow-minded, self-centered, tight-fisted, two-faced, bad-tempered — often negative
💡 Stress: in compound adjectives like these, the stress usually falls on the second word — absent-MINDed, self-CENTered, tight-FISTed.
